An AI photoshoot studio for Bharat's sellers. One phone photo in — a catalog, model shots, vernacular festive creatives, and a 10-second reel out. Directed by conversation, in the seller's own language.

User Flow — where each model comes in

The persona is Lakshmi, a saree weaver in Dharmavaram who sells on WhatsApp and Meesho. She speaks Telugu, has one phone photo of her saree, and no design skills. Design principle: the chat is never blocked — slow work (reel, Drive sync) runs in the background while she reviews.

In-chat approvals + multi-turn edits ("పెళ్లి వేదిక దగ్గర మోడల్ మీద చూపించు")

Gemini 3.5 Flash NB2 multi-turn chain /api/edit

Step A — Director: 3.5 Flash converts the seller's plain Telugu/Hinglish instruction into an optimized English image-editing prompt, with two hard rules: never alter the product's colors/patterns/textures, and if on-image text is requested, state the exact text first, then instruct accurate script rendering (the docs-recommended text technique[12]).

Step B — Render: the first edit on a chosen image sends that image + prompt to NB2; every follow-up sends only text plus previous_interaction_id, so Google carries the visual state across turns — the docs-recommended multi-turn editing pattern[12]. Picking a different base image resets the chain. NB2 (not Lite) runs edits because Lite is documented as "not optimized for multi-turn sequential editing," while NB2 carries "reliable text rendering"[12]. Each result renders inline in the chat with Approve / Edit-again controls.

/api/approve · Drive Approved/

On approve, the asset is (a) marked in the session's approved set, (b) copied to Drive <product>/Approved/ in the background, and (c) exposed to the storefront API. If the approved hero differs from the image the background reel was seeded with, the chat offers one tap: "Re-animate from this shot?" — no silent stale reels. Drive is framed as the seller's asset library: Lakshmi owns her whole campaign in her own Drive.

Storefront page · /api/store/{session}

A polished single product page (same backend, separate route) polls the approved-assets endpoint: approved images fill the gallery, the Omni Flash reel autoplays as the hero, vernacular festive creative sits in the promo slot. On stage: cut from the chat to the storefront — "and this is Lakshmi's store, five minutes after one photo." No cart, no checkout — one page, done well.

Solo-builder cut order if time slips: Drive sync goes first (show a pre-populated folder and say "assets sync to the seller's Drive"), reel re-animation offer second. The storefront is never cut — it is the demo climax. The chat + angles + edits pipeline is the non-negotiable core.

India Impact — the research

1 · The problem is priced out of reach

Professional product photography in India costs roughly ₹300–₹800 per basic image, climbing to ₹1,500–₹8,000 per image for premium retouched work[1][2]. Per-product e-commerce rates run ₹500–₹2,500 even in bulk (100+ products)[3]. A 50-product catalog at 4 angles each lands around ₹60,000, and adding on-model shots can push totals past ₹1.5 lakh[4]. For a seller whose typical listing sells at ₹300–₹600, that math simply never closes — so most list a single dim phone photo and lose the click to branded competitors.

PhotoDukaan's economics: ~₹3 per generated image (NB2 Lite at $0.034 per 1K image[5]) and ~₹85 per 10-second reel (Omni Flash at ~$0.10/second[13]) — a full campaign for under ₹100, roughly a 99.8% cost reduction versus a conventional shoot.

2 · The people it serves are the fastest-growing sellers in India

MSMEs form India's retail backbone, contributing ~30% of GDP (~$1T annually); they are projected to drive nearly half of incremental e-commerce growth[6]

3 · Vernacular is not a feature — it's the market

57% of Indian internet users prefer Indian languages over English[9], and roughly 9 out of 10 new internet users come online in an Indian language[10]. Yet design tools, prompt boxes, and ad platforms remain English-first. PhotoDukaan inverts that: the seller directs the shoot in Telugu or Hindi, and the output creatives carry accurate native-script text — the single hardest thing for most image models, and NB2's documented strength[12].

4 · The market this unlocks

India's social commerce market is estimated at $29.27B in 2025, forecast to reach $143.86B by 2030 (37.5% CAGR)[15], driven precisely by Tier-2/3 sellers, UPI micro-orders, and vernacular content. McKinsey sizes modular digital-commerce tooling for MSMEs — the category PhotoDukaan sits in — at $25–30B by 2030[6]. And the conversion mechanism is proven: 77% of shoppers say images influence purchase decisions[4], while 22% of e-commerce returns happen because products look different from their photos[16] — better imagery both sells more and returns less.

The one-line impact claim for judges: India has ~60M MSMEs and a million-plus first-time online sellers, most in small towns, most preferring Indian languages — and the single biggest conversion lever, professional product imagery, costs 100–500× what they can pay. PhotoDukaan collapses a ₹60,000 photoshoot into a ₹100, five-minute, Telugu-language conversation.
